# Sound

A source's sound design is recovered from a mixed track, so separation comes first. `toolkit/measure/sound.py` runs Demucs to pull the voice out, then splits what remains into the music bed, which is periodic and sustained, and effects, which are transients and sweeps; it records which method ran, and a fallback separation is noted as a gap because it misses quiet effects under the voice. Check the detections against the spectrogram of the non-voice stem before trusting them, the way move fits are checked against frame strips.

Every effect is written down by what it is bound to. The binding is the hand: which property's move it answers, which keyframe it fires on (start, peak velocity, landing or cut), how far it leads or trails that keyframe, its level under the voice, its timbre and envelope, and whether it pans with the motion. Effects with no visual partner are recorded too, since a hand that scores some beats with sound alone does so deliberately. The style's `references/sound.md` describes the palette and binding rules in prose with the numbers, including what the ear hears that the measures miss, such as a whoosh that is filtered rather than loud or effects that thin out once the voice starts.

The palette in `sound/` serves three kinds of clip in order of preference: clean source clips that pass the isolation check, generated clips from ElevenLabs sound generation scored against the source profile, and generated clips for novel moves built from the binding rule of the nearest property and class. A generated clip is accepted only when its spectral match beats the threshold in `toolkit/measure/sound-bands.json`, and the prompt that produced it is kept beside it so the palette can be regenerated.

Placement follows the plan. `toolkit/sound-plan.py` resolves each move's keyframe time, applies its binding rule, respects any `sfx` override or `sfx: false` in the scene plan, caps density at the source's measured events per ten seconds, and places the music bed with its measured duck under the voice. The plan's effects appear on the storyboard's timing rulers and render as HyperFrames audio clips, and the loop scores their sync, level, class mix and timbre against the source.
