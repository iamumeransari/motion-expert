# Scenes

The source runs 47.4 s, 42 shots between 41 hard cuts, as sixteen beats in four movements, and this file gives the structure without any of its content: each beat with its share of the runtime, how long its holds last, how it enters and leaves, its energy as the mean of the normalised flow-energy curve over the beat (the whole film averages 0.074, and the five peaks sit at 22.0, 23.4, 33.2, 35.3 and 38.6 s), and the feeling it lands. Shot numbers point at `moves.md`. A new piece of a different length repeats this order of beats and scales the number of items inside a beat (more title cards, more tiles, more plates) rather than stretching any one beat, because the holds are measured and a stretched hold reads as a stall.

## Movement one: the catalogue (0 to 10.5 s, 22 percent)

### 1. Word-per-cut titles (shots 1 to 7, 0.0 to 3.3 s, 7 percent, energy 0.039)

Six single serif words, each on its own photographic plate and inside the same four-chip selection, cut every 8 to 15 frames (median 13, about 0.54 s, which sits a little longer than one 140.3 BPM beat of 0.428 s), then the last word steps up in size and is invaded by photo tiles. Each card holds still apart from the six-frame handle glide and tracking snap at its start, so roughly half of every card is a held frame. It enters cold on the first card with no fade and leaves by tiles covering the last card and a hard cut to paper. The feeling is range and confidence: a fast inventory of everything the subject covers, read one word at a time, each word claimed by the same selection.

### 2. Scatter and collapse (shot 8, 3.3 to 5.3 s, 4 percent, energy 0.076)

Photo tiles and lowercase words pop onto grid paper one or two per frame, then the words snap into one line and the tiles clear. It enters on a hard cut with tiles already present and leaves through the glyph scramble. The feeling is abundance being sorted: a mess of material that resolves into one sentence.

### 3. The lockup (shot 8, 5.3 to 6.8 s, 3 percent, energy 0.024)

The scramble resolves into a small centred lockup on plain paper, violet for two frames and then black, and holds still for 26 frames. The feeling is arrival, quiet and certain, the first real breath in the film.

### 4. Type that cannot settle, then the mark (shot 8, 6.8 to 9.0 s, 5 percent, energy 0.015)

The lockup's second word cycles through typefaces on every update while the line scales up past the frame, then a hard change to a single violet tile that builds its mark in steps and collects its handles one by one, each clicked. Energy is low because the moves are steps rather than travel. The feeling is play and precision together: the same name tried in every voice, then pinned down.

### 5. The giant pass (shot 8, 9.0 to 10.5 s, 3 percent, energy 0.221)

A giant word crosses the frame from the lower right while a blurred photograph blooms up behind it and the type turns white where the picture lands. This is the first energy peak's run-up and the film's first big gesture. It leaves on a hard cut to the chromatic plates. The feeling is a swell, the moment the film opens up.

## Movement two: the claim (10.5 to 17.5 s, 15 percent)

### 6. Chromatic plates under a still line (shots 9 to 12, 10.5 to 14.2 s, 8 percent, energy 0.040)

A short white line holds dead still while blurred, colour-split photographs change under it every 4 to 15 frames with occasional two or three frame crossfades, then a hard change to paper where the line takes the plates' colours as gradients and holds for 28 frames (13.0 to 14.2 s). The feeling is a statement made over a light show: the words stay calm while everything behind them shimmers, and the final paper hold says it plainly.

### 7. Footage breath (shots 13 to 16, 14.2 to 17.5 s, 7 percent, energy 0.037)

Four photographic shots, 10 to 25 frames each, with slow camera drift and no graphics at all. The feeling is a reset to the real: after the type, the subject itself, unhurried.

## Movement three: the demonstrations (17.5 to 30.5 s, 27 percent)

### 8. Select, lift and edit (shots 17 to 19, 17.5 to 20.5 s, 6 percent, energy 0.011)

A two-word imperative appears over a held photograph, a cursor drags a selection over the subject, the subject lifts in front of the type, and then a run of clicks changes the background, the clothing and the light while the subject stays fixed. The holds are long (33, 25 and 15 frame shots) and almost nothing travels, which is why the energy is the lowest of any beat. The feeling is control: a calm hand doing something precise.

### 9. Giant type behind the subject, then a push (shots 20 to 24, 20.5 to 22.9 s, 5 percent, energy 0.132)

The next imperative is set giant and sits behind an object in the frame, then vanishes, the frame pushes in 1.47 times, and the object is held while its background swaps on the beat. The feeling is boldness: the type as large as the picture, and the picture willing to be torn out of its setting.

### 10. Rise, shuffle and a label edit (shots 25 and 26, 22.9 to 26.5 s, 8 percent, energy 0.103)

A giant word rising from below over a swapped background, a print-shrink and a card shuffle into the next photograph, a staggered three-word line behind a rising object, and then a cursor click that changes one line of text on the object with a push in. The feeling is momentum with a wink: the edits get smaller and more exact as the pace picks up.

### 11. Type becomes image (shots 27 and 28, 26.5 to 29.25 s, 6 percent, energy 0.054)

A dark grey two-word line on paper, held for 1.75 s while it grows by three percent, then filled with a photograph and echoed above and below, then a cut to printed pages with the line in white stepping down and away. The feeling is anticipation and release: a long hold that pays off in a burst of repeats.

### 12. Footage of the result (shots 29 and 30, 29.25 to 30.5 s, 3 percent, energy 0.053)

Two observational shots of printed output and people looking at it. The feeling is proof, the work out in the world.

## Movement four: the scale-up and the sign-off (30.5 to 47.4 s, 36 percent)

### 13. Big, bigger (shots 31 to 33, 30.5 to 34.0 s, 7 percent, energy 0.046)

A giant ghost word behind tiles that pop in, a small word that grows and is selected, then a single selected photograph that grows in two steps to the full width with its handles, the word growing and tracking open with it. Holds of 14, 13 and 57 frames. The feeling is escalation by steps, each step a decision.

### 14. Oops, print and the street (shots 34 to 37, 34.0 to 36.75 s, 4 percent, energy 0.153)

A short interjection set over a photograph, then the picture shrinks to a print and lands on a billboard in the street. The feeling is a laugh followed by scale: a small mistake becomes a public image.

### 15. Billboard run (shots 38 to 41, 36.75 to 41.4 s, 11 percent, energy 0.197)

Billboards and posters in different streets and lights, cut every 13 to 20 frames, ending on the last billboard with a one-second pull-back that halves its size and then holds for ten frames. This is the energy peak of the film. The feeling is reach and triumph: the image everywhere at once.

### 16. End card (shot 42, 41.4 to 47.4 s, 13 percent, energy 0.020)

The music drops out, a one-line sentence types on inside violet handles, the handles collapse onto the mark, the name slides out beside it and a small line appears underneath; the card then holds still for 3 s. It enters on a hard cut from the billboard and ends on the hold with no fade. The feeling is the exhale: quiet, final, the selection resolving into a signature.

## The arc

Energy reads as a slow-burning curve with three swells (9 to 10.5 s, 20.5 to 26.5 s, and 34 to 41.4 s, each higher than the last) separated by near-still graphic beats, and a cold, still ending. Graphic beats on paper are always the calm ones and photographic beats are always the energetic ones, so the pacing of a new piece comes from alternating the two grounds. Hard cuts carry 85 percent of transitions; the soft ones belong only to the giant pass, the chromatic plates and the night posters.
