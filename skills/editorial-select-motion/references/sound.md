# Sound

## Separation and what it can be trusted for

`toolkit/measure/sound.py` ran Demucs (htdemucs) on the mix. There is no voice in the source: the "voice" stem measures -40.4 LUFS and covers 10.6 percent of the runtime, which is bleed from the music's own mid-range and not speech, so every level the fingerprint quotes as dB relative to the voice is really relative to that bleed and is only useful for ranking events against each other. The music bed sits at -10.8 LUFS and is present for the whole film except the drop at 41.4 to 42.5 s. The fingerprint's 84 events (55 pops, 14 clicks, 8 other, 3 sub-drops, 2 chimes, 2 impacts, 17.7 per 10 s) were checked against a spectrogram of the full mix with every cut marked (`assets/frames/spectrogram-*.png`), and most of them are the music's own percussion rather than designed effects laid on the picture. That reading is the main gap in this file: the designed effects and the track share one stem, and the separation cannot pull them apart. Clean clips for the palette are left to `toolkit/sfx.py`.

## What the spectrogram shows

The track is a 136 BPM electronic cue (beat 0.441 s) built in sections that follow the picture's four movements. From 0 to 3.3 s, over the title cards, it is a dense run of broadband transient hits, one roughly on each word cut. From 3.3 to 5.5 s, over the grid scatter, it thins to narrow, high ticks (the fingerprint's clicks at 3.92, 4.00 and 4.12 s, centroid about 5.7 kHz, 4.5 ms attack, 18 ms decay), one per tile or word pop. From 5.5 to 13 s a driving four-on-the-floor section carries the lockup, the mark and the chromatic plates, and two downward pitch glides at about 7.6 and 8.3 s sit against the mark tile's size steps. From 13 to 22 s the drums fall away to sustained tonal pads with two more descending glides at about 17.1 and 17.8 s, under the footage and the select-and-edit demonstrations, so the calm graphic beats get the quietest music. Percussion returns sparsely from 22 to 27 s, a broadband noise swell starts at about 28.2 s exactly as the type fills with image and echoes, and from 28.6 to 41.4 s the track runs at full density under the spreads, the growing selection and the billboards. At 41.4 s it stops dead on the cut to the end card, and from 42.5 to 45.5 s a few dry tonal plucks answer the handle collapse and the name reveal before the card holds in near silence.

## The palette by class

Pops are the bulk (median attack 7 ms, decay 42 ms, centroid 675 Hz, duration 51 ms) and are mostly the track's kick and percussion. Clicks are short and bright (attack 4.5 ms, decay 18 ms, centroid 5.7 kHz); six isolated cleanly enough to be kept as clips by `measure/sound.py` (events 7, 8, 9, 42, 43 and 79) and they are the closest thing the source has to designed interface sounds, sitting on tile pops, the edit clicks at 22.48 and 22.74 s and the end card. Sub-drops (centroid 122 Hz, decay 256 ms) land at 11.06, 23.33 and 28.29 s, on the plate change, the card shuffle and the image fill. Impacts (centroid 330 Hz, decay 302 ms) land at 32.58 s on the selection's growth step and at 38.76 s at the start of the long pull-back. Chimes (centroid 980 Hz, attack 134 ms, decay 272 ms) sit at 5.48 s on the lockup's arrival and at 15.09 s in the footage. The "other" class (centroid 620 Hz, short) clusters around the cursor clicks at 18.47 to 21.12 s and the end card plucks at 45.39 and 45.50 s.

## Binding rules the fingerprint supports

Sixty-two events bind to a visual keyframe within 250 ms, with a median absolute offset of 63 ms against a chance level of 104 ms, so sound and picture are tied but loosely. The rules with more than three instances are these: a pop on a cut, 5 ms early, eight times (23 percent of cuts); a pop on a translate's peak velocity, 25 ms late, seven times; a pop on a translate's start, 17 ms late, five times; a pop on a scale's landing, 24 ms early, four times; and a pop on a translate's landing, 120 ms late, four times. Only 34 percent of cuts land within 70 ms of a beat, so the picture is cut to the phrase and the energy of the track rather than locked to the grid. For a new piece the useful rules are: put a tick on every tile or word pop and every handle landing, a sub-drop on the beat where type turns into image or a card shuffles, an impact under the selection's biggest growth step and under the start of a long pull-back, and let the music carry everything else.

## The music bed

A driving electronic cue at 136 BPM, music only, with sections that rise and fall with the picture rather than one level throughout: dense hits under the title cards, ticks under the scatter, drive under the lockup and the plates, pads under footage and demonstrations, drive again from 28.6 s, and a hard stop at the end card. There is nothing to duck, because there is no voice. When a piece in this style carries the house voice, the bed should sit under it by the margin `toolkit/sound-plan.py` uses by default and keep its sectional shape, since the drop into the end card is part of the hand.

## What the ear hears that the measures miss

The cuts feel on the beat even though only a third measure on it, because the title cards and billboards land on the strongest hits and the in-between cuts land on off-beat percussion. The pitch glides are the only effects that clearly belong to the picture (they slide down as the mark tile steps and as the selection is dragged) and they are quiet, sitting well under the drums. The end card's silence is the loudest thing in the film.
