The serif is 205TF Exposure (Federico Parra Barrios, 2022), set at different points on its exposure axis per scene. The match comes from 205TF's own specimen PDF, whose embedded subsets of every exposure step were rendered glyph by glyph over the film's own letter positions and scored by the house SSIM (960w, 1.0 prefilter, Wang window) in the word area: Exposure -20 scores 0.980 on "will never be the same" where the best open serif reaches 0.936, Exposure +10 scores 0.993 on "Try it now at ideogram.ai" against 0.953, and the one full subset in the specimen, Exposure 0, averages 0.934 over six paper lines, above every open face even on lines where its weight is wrong. Measured against Exposure 0, almost every line in the film sits about 9.5 units heavier per edge, which is where -20 falls, while "Big" and the end-card URL sit at 0 to +10. The speckled edges are not in the outlines, which are smooth in the specimen, so they come from the edit's grain and print treatment. Exposure is commercial, so nothing here ships it. Of 27 open serifs fitted the same way across their weight, optical size and softness axes, the best means over six lines are Source Serif 4 0.909 (opsz 17, wght 550), Crimson Pro 0.902, Merriweather 0.891, Literata 0.887 and Petrona 0.886 (the older 0.61 to 0.64 figure was raw full-resolution SSIM on the s1 step frames, a different measure). The style therefore uses its own face, EditorialSelect Serif in assets/fonts, with 36 glyphs traced from the film at the -20 weight and 65 filled from Source Serif 4 at matched stems and sidebearings (coverage in EditorialSelect-Serif-coverage.md). Set at the film's measured glyph positions it scores 0.991 on "Launch it", 0.979 on "Introducing Ideogram 4.5", 0.973 on "Model weights are coming soon" and 0.977 on "will never be the same"; set by word, with its own advances, kerning and each line's measured tracking, it scores 0.987, 0.960, 0.952 and 0.931, the last because the s1 sentence was set with tracking the per-line term does not fully absorb. Letters the film never shows (f j q x z, fourteen capitals, most figures and the punctuation) are Source Serif 4 shapes, so they read as the same family but not as Exposure.
