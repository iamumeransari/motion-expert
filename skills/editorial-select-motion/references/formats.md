# Formats

## 16:9, the source (1920 x 1080, 24 fps)

Every measured value in this skill is for 1920 x 1080 and every element scales its pixel values by the frame width over 1920. The title word sits centred with its baseline at 0.547 of the height and a cap height of 0.094 of the height; the selection's chips sit 34 px outside the ink and 59 px above the cap line and below the baseline at 102 px cap height, and those offsets scale with the cap height. Grid paper is ruled every 120 px across and 121.5 px down through the frame centre. Margins in the graphic beats are wide (the fingerprint's measured safe margins are 3 to 6 percent at the sides) because type sits in the middle and photographs fill the frame edge to edge. The picture updates sixteen times a second inside the 24 fps file (frames with index mod 3 equal to 2 repeat the one before), which is done once at the end of a render, not in the elements.

## 9:16 (1080 x 1920), derived and not yet proven by a piece

Keep the timing unit, the curves, the cadence and the energy shape, and re-derive layout as shares of the frame. Title words keep their cap height as a share of the frame's width rather than its height (102 / 1920 of the width, so about 57 px at 1080 wide), since a single word must fit across; longer words drop to the largest cap height that leaves 0.08 of the width clear each side for the chips. The selection box keeps its proportions to the cap height. The grid keeps 120 px cells measured on the 1920 side scaled to the new width (67.5 px), so the paper reads at the same density. Tiles scatter in a taller field, so a scatter beat needs about 1.6 times as many tiles to reach the same density. Photographic plates are cropped to the subject's centre rather than letterboxed, and the giant type beats (giant pass, rising word, ghost word) are set so one word fills about 0.9 of the width and runs off the top or bottom rather than the sides. The end card stays centred, with the lockup and its handles at the same share of the width.

## 1:1 (1080 x 1080), derived and not yet proven by a piece

Layout follows the 9:16 rules for width (cap height and chip offsets as shares of the width) and the 16:9 rules for vertical placement (title baseline at 0.547 of the height). Giant type runs off both sides as in the source. Scatter beats need about 1.3 times as many tiles as the source.

New formats are added here once a piece in them has passed the loop, with what changed and why.
