# The sound layer

Sound is part of the hand. Which sound fires on which move, how far it leads or trails the keyframe, how loud it sits under the voice, its timbre and its envelope transfer to a new piece exactly, the way a curve or a palette does, and a move the source never made still gets a sound from inside the source's sonic grammar. Five steps carry that from a found video to a rendered piece: capture, palette, generation, placement and validation.

```
measure/sound.py <video> --moves <motion|fingerprint>.json --out sound.json [--stems-dir DIR] [--plot spec.png] [--device cpu|mps]
music.py measure <video|audio> --out music.json [--stems-dir DIR] [--beats-out beats.json] [--plain]
music.py generate <music.json> --duration S --out-dir DIR [--n 2] [--sections intro,build,peak] [--dry-run]
music.py score <music.json> <audio>
sfx.py palette <sound.json> --style-dir DIR [--music music.json]
sfx.py generate DIR --class C [--target JSON|file] [--n 4] [--prompt TEXT] [--dry-run]
sfx.py pick DIR --move PROPERTY [--class C] [--key start|peak|land|cut]
sfx.py analyse <clip>
sound-plan.py <scene-plan.json> --style-dir DIR --out sfx-plan.json [--music bed] [--beats grid.json] [--snap-beats 0.06] [--vo vo.wav]
```

## Capture

`measure/sound.py` splits the mix with Demucs htdemucs (CPU by default, which is bit-reproducible; stems cached per source under `--stems-dir/<hash>/`). It first asks whether there is a voice at all, since a music-only source still leaves a faint, garbled vocal stem: the voice counts when its stem is louder than -38 LUFS and within 12 dB of the bed. With a voice, effects are searched in the non-vocal stem plus the vocal stem wherever nobody is speaking, because Demucs files isolated clicks and ticks under vocals. Without one the whole mix is searched. Every level is quoted against a reference named in `level_ref`: the voice's 50 ms p90 power when there is a voice, the bed's when there is not. `level_db_rel_mix` is always present as well, so two sources with different references still compare.

Candidates are onsets of the excess over a running 1.2 s median bed, judged per band so a duck releasing or the bed entering does not count. Each candidate is then tested against the loop: the share of its energy that the non-vocal stem also holds one beat, half a bar, a bar, two or four bars away (level-matched, phase-free) decides whether it is music. A second pass relies on editors reusing samples: a sharp transient under speech that matches another sample for sample, off the beat grid, is an effect even when Demucs filed it as voice, and every accepted effect becomes a template matched across the whole mix. Timing comes from a 2 ms band-limited envelope for the onset and a 20 ms one for peak and end, so a click keeps millisecond timing and a whoosh is not ended by its first ripple. The class (whoosh, riser, impact, click, pop, glitch, chime, typing, sub-drop, other) comes from soft rules over duration, attack, decay, amplitude-weighted centroid, tonality (share of power in spectral peaks), low-end share, sweep and the count and regularity of separate hits, calibrated on the media-use library, where 15 of 19 samples land in their class.

Each event is bound to the nearest move keyframe (start, peak velocity or landing from `motion.py`) or cut within 0.25 s. Transient classes bind their onset and sweep classes their peak, and `offset_to_move_s` is always onset minus anchor, so placement puts the clip's first sample at anchor plus offset. Dense move lists bind anything by accident, so `sync.chance_median_abs_s` records what evenly spaced random times would score against the same anchors; binding means something only when the median absolute offset sits well under it. An event isolated from speech and from other events, standing 12 dB over the bed in its own bands, is cut out with a Wiener mask against the local bed spectrum and written to `clips/event_###.wav` with `clean: true`.

The bed's context is measured here too: integrated LUFS with every effect cut out, tempo, the duck as the median drop at speech-to-pause boundaries, and how cuts sit against the beat. `cuts_on_beat_share` is reported with `cuts_on_beat_chance`, because at 136 BPM a ±70 ms window catches 32% of random cuts, and `cuts_beat_lock` is the phase-free concentration of cut times within the beat (1 locked, about 0.15 random for 35 cuts). `music.py measure` describes the bed itself: beats and downbeats (the beat phase carrying the most kick energy plus chord change), bars, sections clustered from per-bar loudness, brightness, low end, density and chroma, the energy arc, drops and breaks, key with its confidence, stem shares and a plain-words description, and it exports `beats.json` in the shape `validate-plan.mjs` reads, so scene plans can time moves as `beat:<n>` or `downbeat:<n>`.

## Palette

`sfx.py palette` turns `sound.json` into `<style>/sound/palette.json`: one entry per class holding the measured target (median and p10 to p90 range of duration, centroid, bandwidth, attack, decay, level and pan), the clean source clips copied into `sound/source/`, and the binding rules with each rule's coverage, the share of that property's moves that had a sound in the source. A clean source clip is reference audio and is used as is in new pieces, because the sound is part of the hand and not the source's content. `palette.json` also carries `move_map`, which the capturing agent fills from `moves.md` with each library move's property, duration and peak fraction, so placement does not have to guess them from move names.

## Generation

Classes without a clean clip, and novel moves, get generated sound. `sfx.py generate` asks ElevenLabs for four candidates at spread prompt influence, trims, measures and scores each against the class target (and against the clean source clip by MFCC when one exists), and keeps the best in `sound/generated/` with its score. `music.py generate` builds a music_v2_5 composition plan from the bed profile, keeps the best of n by score, and writes the bed's own beat grid next to it. Prompting notes are in `SFX.md`.

## Placement

`sound-plan.py` reads the scene plan and the palette and writes the `sfx-plan.json` that `lib/sfx.mjs` loads for the board and the build. Grammar-composed moves give their property and keyframes exactly (tween `at` and `units` in the style's timing unit, peak velocity from the easing's bezier); library moves use `move_map`, else the move name and a four-unit default, flagged `estimated_keyframes`. Each move takes its best-supported rule, and coverage is honoured by error diffusion so a property sounded on a fifth of its moves in the source is sounded on a fifth here, without randomness. A property the source never sounded takes the style's dominant rule at the source's overall sounded share. The density cap keeps any 10 s window under the source's density plus a quarter, dropping the least-supported events first. Gain is computed rather than guessed: the target level against the reference is reproduced against this piece's voice-over (or its bed when the source had no voice), both measured in the same units as capture, and the bed's gain keeps the source's bed-to-voice loudness offset and duck. `--snap-beats` moves events within the given distance onto the bed's beats. HyperFrames has no pan effect, so `build-composition.mjs` bakes pan into a copy of the clip; per-clip start and level are `data-start` and `data-volume` (linear, 0 to 3.98), and the duck is a gain lane in `data-automation`.

## Validation

`compare.py` scores the render's `sound` block against the source's, with scalar bands from `measure/sound-bands.json` merged into the fingerprint's tolerance block and per-class bands from its own `SOUND` constants. The metrics are event density per 10 s (±25%), the sync offset distribution (median ±30 ms, p10 and p90 ±50 ms, and per class), level against the reference (±3 dB overall and per class), the class mix (total variation 0.25 for a novel piece, 0.15 for a replica), per-class spectral match (centroid within 0.3 octave, duration within ×1.33, attack and decay within ×1.5, bound property share ±0.25), and the bed (LUFS ±2, tempo ±4%, duck ±3 dB, beat lock ±0.2). Classes with fewer than three source events are reported rather than scored.

## How well it measures

On a synthetic fixture with known truth (the goals voice-over, the prism bed ducked as `mix.py` does it, and 20 library effects at -6 to -14 dB against the voice, 13 of them under speech), timing, level and pan are good where an event is found: onset within 10 ms median, level within 1 dB, pan within 0.05, and binding keys and offsets recovered to 8 ms. Detection is the weak part, at 0.25 recall and 0.45 precision. The misses are mostly effects under speech, which Demucs files under vocals in full, and effects coinciding with a loop hit, which the music test then explains away. On the goals reel, both clicks a matched filter confirms in the mix are found to the millisecond (5.231 s and 25.463 s, both found by sample reuse), alongside 12 other events, about a third of which are bed onsets. On the prism bed alone, with no effects at all, it reports 7 events in 40 s, all false. On a generated ElevenLabs bed it reports none.

So a capture is trustworthy for how the found effects sound and sit, and loose on how many there are. The capturing agent reads the `--plot` spectrogram (events dashed and labelled, clean clips starred, speech as a blue strip, move keyframes as ticks) and prunes events that sit on the loop's grid before the palette is built.

## Gaps

Effects under continuous speech are missed unless they repeat sample for sample, because Demucs routes them into the vocal stem. Four fixes were tried and measured, and none is in: TIGER-DnR (a dialogue, music and effects separator) took 325 s for 8 s of audio on this machine and returned an empty effects stem; PANNs AudioSet event detection gave effect classes probabilities near 0.003 under a voice and a bed; a second Demucs pass on the vocal stem kept the effects in vocals; and subtracting the ElevenLabs voice isolator's output (`/v1/audio-isolation`, which the key allows) raised effects only 0 to 6 dB over the speech residue, because the isolator re-synthesises the voice. Telling a varied loop from an effect laid on it has no clean rule either, since drum fills and section changes leave as much unexplained energy as a real effect. Slow tonal glides (a pitch sweep with no noise in it) are not tracked as events. And keyframes for library moves are estimated until `move_map` is filled.
